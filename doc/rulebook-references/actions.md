List of actions:
Actions:
Simple Actions:  
These actions are things such as limited movement.  Simple actions labeled with [Dramatic] can be done on that character’s turn while that character is KO’d.

Step: [Dramatic] The most basic simple action.  This movement allows a character to shift up to 3 meters.

Low Tier Ability:  Activating or using an ability that is two tiers under the character’s tier is a simple action.

Take a position:  Gaining the advantage of cover, dropping prone, or standing up are examples of positions that are a simple action.
	
Take Cover: [Dramatic] This action gives the character a +2 bonus to DEF/MDF.  It requires some kind of obstacle to be behind.  If the obstacle blocks more than half the view of the character, the bonus is increased to +4 instead.

Prone: Dropping prone involves lying on the ground to reduce the target area of a character’s body, improving the likeliness that attacks from a distance will miss the character.  Being prone gives a +4 bonus to DEF/MDF against ranged attacks.  It comes at a downside against melee attackers though, as they gain a +2 bonus to hit a prone target.

Assist: [Dramatic] You can move to assist another character at performing an action as a free action.  This can be used for a number of purposes, and flavored as flanking(for a to hit bonus), aiding in defense (a DEF or MDF bonus), etc.  Assisting gives a +1 bonus for that round.  The character you are assisting must be within 10m of you.

Enter a Style: Gain the benefit of a Style.  Styles have specific requirements to activate.  These requirements are explained in the Styles section.

Maintain a Stance: A character can maintain a stance as a simple action.  Stance benefits are explained in the Standard actions section. You cannot be prone and maintain a stance.

Standard Actions: 
These actions take a small amount of effort and time to perform.   A character may also move up to 6 meters, make a basic melee or ranged attack, or activate a skill or spell that is labeled as a standard action.  Skills and spells that are equivalent to the user’s tier are standard actions.  Using a consumable is also considered a standard action.  

Activate a Skill:  These are powerful arts that consume MP to perform various combo attacks that can be elementally charged, or inflict various status effects.  Attack skills must meet or exceed the target’s DEF score to hit.  These skills often have a power rating, which is added to the character’s POW to determine the damage of the attack.

Cast a Spell:  These are magical abilities that allow the caster to inflict harm on an opponent, or heal wounds and provide other support to allies or themselves.  Spells consume MP to cast.  All attack spells have an elemental quality, and some deal only status effects instead of damage.  To successfully hit with a spell, a roll must be made that meets or exceeds the target’s MDF.  Damaging spells have a power rating that is added to the caster’s MAG to determine the damage of the spell.  When casting an AoE spell, a roll to hit must be made against every valid target’s MDF.

Status spells inflict a status effect on the target(s).  A cast roll of 1d20 + the character’s Tier bonus is applied against the targets’ MDF.  If successful, the intended status effect is inflicted.  The information on all status effects is on the Abilities SRD.

Support spells grant a benefit, or heal injuries. These spells are assumed to always succeed, and never need a to-hit roll to activate.

Run: A character can move up to 6 meters, or jump up to 4 meters as a standard action. (or any combination thereof)

Movement Skills:  These are unique abilities that allow characters to perform special maneuvers, like jump unnaturally high, glide, or slide for extra movement distance.  

Interact with Environment: This can be a large number of things, but typically it involves opening or closing a door, disabling an alarm, or interacting with an NPC (in the case of escorting a VIP, etc)

Enter a Form:  These powerful transformations are described in detail in the Forms section.

Take a Stance: Gain the benefit of a Stance.  Stances have various bonuses and penalties.  A character is never considered to ‘always be in a stance’, so one must always be entered by spending a standard action.  Maintaining a stance costs a simple action each turn.
	
	Aggressive Stance: +2 bonus to hit, -2 penalty to DEF and MDF
	Berserk Stance: +2 bonus to damage, -2 penalty to DEF and MDF
	Defensive Stance: +2 bonus to DEF, -2 penalty to hit
	Focused Stance: +2 MDF, -2 penalty to hit

Taunt: [Dramatic] Throw an insult at an enemy to try to distract them.  Grant one target a -1 penalty to hit, DEF, or MDF for a single round.  You must be within 10m of the target to taunt them, they must be able to see or hear you.  This action can be performed as a dramatic action.  This penalty can stack up to a total penalty of -3.

Initiate a D-Link:  This action allows a character to initiate a destiny link with a friend that is not currently present with a FL of at least 2.  This link lasts for 3 rounds.  D-Link offers all the benefits of fighting with that friend with one extra benefit.  While D-Linked with a friend, the character has access to any skills the friend has Mark of Mastery for.

Draw: This action allows a character to regain 2 MP.
Complex Actions:  
These are incredible abilities that require an extended amount of focus or concentration to perform. Activating a skill or spell that is up to 1 Tier higher than them, moving up to 10 meters, activating a Summon, Shotlock, Link skill, or recovering and using a stowed item are all  complex actions.

All Out Attack: A character can perform an All Out Attack, which grants a +4 to hit, as a complex action.  This action doesn’t affect defense in any way, but it does use up extra action economy, which limits other options in that turn.  This can only be used on basic attacks.  
	
Incantation: By uttering a verbal incantation before casting a spell, or using a skill, the character can gain a +4 to hit.  This type of action increases the difficulty of casting the spell by one step.  A Simple action becomes a standard action, or a standard action becomes a complex action. 

Charge: Rush toward a target and deliver a single attack (basic or attack ability).  Gain a one point of bonus damage on that attack for every four meters of movement in a charge.  This action can also be taken with leaping or diving attacks.  A single charge action can gain benefits from movement up to the character's base movement value.  This rule does not apply to leaping or diving attacks.

Grand Power: Casting a spell or activating an ability that is one Tier higher than the caster can be performed as a complex action.

	Sprint: A character can sprint up to 10 meters as a complex action.
	
Link Skills: These powerful abilities are learned once a character has a friendship link of 3.  You get one link skill with each Friendship Link.  Link skills can only be performed while you are in tandem combat with the friend you learned it with.  Some link skills are labeled Trinity Skills. These require a trio of friends to perform, and are extremely powerful.  Link skills are a complex action to perform.  The MP cost for Link Skills is split between the users.  This split cost does not need to be split evenly.

Summons: Call forth powerful allies from lost worlds.  These spells always count as the summoner’s Tier for power and MP cost.  Summoning takes time and focus, so it is a complex action to perform.

Shotlocks: Special ranged attacks used with the keyblade, these allow the wielder to focus on one or more targets and fire a barrage of energy blasts at them.  Shotlocks take immense focus, and thus are a complex action to perform.  The Tier, and thus the MP cost of a Shotlock is based on the Tier of the user.  Shotlocks can deal up to 1d6 hits.  Each hit deals half damage.

Use a stowed Item: Items in excess of the characters ‘belt’ slots can be accessed from a backpack, etc as a complex action.  The item can be accessed and used as part of the same action.
